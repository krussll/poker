## Texas Hold'em Broadway Hands: Complete Odds & Domination Hub

In poker, **Broadway hands** consist of any two cards ranked **10, Jack, Queen, King, or Ace**. 

Because broadway cards have the highest raw rank value, they comprise the vast majority of starting hands played in both cash games and tournaments. However, their primary risk is **kicker domination**—holding a lower kicker that loses massive pots to higher broadway holdings.

---

## 1. Broadway Hand Categories

| Tier | Hands | Strategic Profile |
| :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Elite Broadway** | **AKs, AKo** | **"Big Slick."** Highest non-paired hands in poker. Strong pre-flop 3-betting and 4-betting assets. |
| **Premium Suited Broadway** | **AQs, KQs, AJs** | Highly playable; capable of flopping strong top pairs with second-nut or nut flush draws. |
| **Mid Broadways** | **AQo, KJs, QJs, JTs, ATs** | Strong open-raising hands; play cautiously against early-position re-raises. |
| **Marginal / Offsuit Broadways** | **AJo, KQo, ATo, KJo, QJo, KTo** | Prone to reverse-implied odds; easy to be dominated by higher broadways ($AK$, $AQ$). |

## 2. The Math of Kicker Domination

When two players hold hands sharing the same top card, the player with the lower kicker is in **severe mathematical danger**:

- **AK vs. AQ (Dominating Ace)**: $AK$ holds approximately **74% equity** against $AQ$. The dominated player needs to find a 3-outer Queen (approx. 12% on the flop) or straight/flush runner-runners to win.
- **AK vs. AJ**: $AK$ holds approximately **75% equity** over $AJ$.
- **KQ vs. AQ (Dominating Queen)**: $AQ$ holds **70% equity** over $KQ$.
- **KQ vs. AK (Dominating King)**: $AK$ holds **74% equity** over $KQ$.

## 4. Key Rules for Playing Broadway Holdings

1. **Beware the "Trap Hands"**: Offsuit broadways like $K\spadesuit J\diamondsuit$ and $Q\varpi T\clubsuit$ are frequent money-losers when called out of position. Fold them against tight early-position openers.
2. **Suitedness Adds ~3.5% Equity**: A suited broadway ($AKs$) enjoys roughly 3.5% higher equity over its offsuit counterpart ($AKo$) due to backdoor and direct flush frequency.
3. **Control Pot Size with Top Pair Weak Kicker**: When you flop a pair with $AJo$ or $KTo$, aim for small-to-medium pot control rather than playing for all your chips.
