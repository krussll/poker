## What Is the Sklansky Hand Rating System?

The **Sklansky Hand Grouping System** was introduced by legendary poker theorist David Sklansky in his seminal book, *Hold'em Poker for Advanced Players*. 

Before Sklansky's system, many players evaluated starting hands based purely on their high-card strength. Sklansky was among the first to prove mathematically that hand strength is deeply tied to **table position**, **playability across multiple streets**, and the ability to win large pots when hitting strong hidden hands (such as sets and flushes).

Sklansky classified Texas Hold'em starting hands into **8 distinct groups**, with Group 1 being the absolute strongest and Group 8 representing marginal, highly speculative holdings.

## The 8 Sklansky Hand Groups

Below is the complete breakdown of Texas Hold'em starting hands across Sklansky's eight ranking tiers:

| Sklansky Group | Starting Hands Included | Strategic Playing Advice |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Group 1** | **AA, KK, QQ, AKs, JJ** | Premium monster hands. Raise and re-raise from any position; capable of stacking off pre-flop. |
| **Group 2** | **TT, AQs, AJs, KQs, AKo** | Very strong holdings. Standard open-raise from any position; re-raise against loose openers. |
| **Group 3** | **99, JTs, QJs, KJs, ATs, AQo** | Strong broadways and middle pairs. Open from middle/late position; call raises with implied odds. |
| **Group 4** | **T9s, KQo, 88, QTs, 98s, J9s, AJo, KTs** | Good speculative hands and medium pairs. Best played in late position or multi-way pots. |
| **Group 5** | **77, 87s, Q9s, T8s, KJo, QJo, JTo, 76s, 97s, A9s, A8s...** | Suited connectors and small pairs. High implied odds; fold to heavy aggressive action. |
| **Group 6** | **66, 55, 65s, 54s, K9s, J8s, 75s, Q8s, A5s, A4s, A3s, A2s** | Speculative suited aces and low connectors. Profitable from the cutoff and button when unopened. |
| **Group 7** | **44, 33, 22, ATo, KTo, QTo, J8s, 64s, 53s, 43s, T7s...** | Small pocket pairs ("set mining") and weak offsuit broadways. Generally fold in early position. |
| **Group 8** | **86s, 75s, 52s, K9o, Q9o, J8o, 63s, 96s, 85s...** | Very weak speculative hands. Defend selectively in the big blind against min-raises. |

## Why Sklansky Ratings Matter for Pre-Flop Equity

When using our [heads-up poker matchup database](/all-hands), you will notice that hands with a **lower Sklansky rating number** generally dominate hands with higher numbers. 

However, two critical factors must be taken into account when applying Sklansky's framework to modern games:

### 1. Limit vs. No-Limit Texas Hold'em
Sklansky originally developed these rankings for **Limit Texas Hold'em**, where betting amounts are strictly capped on each street. In **No-Limit Hold'em**, small pocket pairs (Group 7) and suited connectors (Groups 5–6) gain significant value because of **implied odds**—the potential to win an opponent's entire stack when you hit a disguised set or straight.

### 2. Heads-Up vs. Full-Ring Dynamics
In a heads-up or short-handed scenario, starting requirements loosen dramatically:
- In full-ring (9 players), an early-position open typically requires a **Group 1 or Group 2** hand.
- In heads-up play, even **Group 6 and Group 7** hands become strong opening candidates because you are guaranteed position on every post-flop betting round.

## Key Strategic Takeaways

1. **Position Multiplies Strength**: A Group 4 hand on the Button is often more profitable to play than a Group 2 hand under-the-gun (UTG).
2. **Beware of Reverse Implied Odds**: Hands like $KQ$ offsuit or $AJ$ offsuit (Groups 3–4) can easily be dominated by higher kickers ($AK$, $AQ$) in large pots.
3. **Use Equities in Conjunction**: Always cross-reference a hand's Sklansky tier with its heads-up equity against opponent ranges using our [Poker Matchup Guides](/all-hands).
